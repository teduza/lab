01 — Mandate & Governance

About the Laboratory

An industrial research unit established to solve local inference, persistent memory, and deterministic edge reliability for autonomous companion systems.

David Sargsyan AI Research Lab was founded on November 13, 2026 as the internal research and development division of M.A.R.S. COMPANION LLC, a technology company incorporated in Kapan, Syunik Province, Republic of Armenia.

The laboratory is named in honor of David Sargsyan, the Father of the company's founder. Its engineering mandate centers on foundational methods that allow voice pipelines, reasoning, and long-term semantic memory to operate reliably on consumer-accessible edge hardware without external compute clusters.

02 — Research Tracks

Six Core Investigations

Every track addresses a concrete engineering constraint required to run persistent, multi-modal AI companions without remote cloud infrastructure.

01

On-Device Reasoning & Sequential Resource Orchestration

Deterministic execution scheduling that coordinates speech recognition, language reasoning, and speech synthesis sequentially within strict RAM and thermal envelopes on compact edge hardware.

02

Long-Term Semantic Memory

Structured local memory architectures that retain facts, context, and longitudinal continuity across months of interaction directly on device storage without external vector clouds.

03

Hardware-Level Privacy

System isolation guarantees where audio streams, semantic indices, and inference states remain confined to user-owned silicon with zero outbound network channels required for operation.

04

Offline Multi-Agent Coordination

Deterministic local message passing and role handoffs between specialized autonomous sub-agents running on local hardware without cloud message brokers.

05

Persona & Identity Systems

Coherent behavioral grounding, voice continuity, and persistent character architectures—forming the foundation for specialized systems including V.E.N.U.S.

06

Fail-Safe Edge, Cold-Boot & Recovery

Atomic memory checkpoints, predictable cold-boot initialization, and autonomous state recovery following power interruption or constrained hardware faults.
